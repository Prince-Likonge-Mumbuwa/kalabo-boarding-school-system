# Biology figures: what ECZ asks, what the syllabus needs, what we can draw

Studied in November 2026 to plan the Biology diagrams for the AI assessment
generator:

- the O Level Biology syllabus, Forms 1–4 (CDC 2024, all 65 pages);
- nine ECZ Biology papers with their marking schemes:
  - Paper 1 (multiple choice): 2022, 2022 GCE, 2024, 2025, 2026 GCE;
  - Paper 2 (theory): 2022, 2022 GCE, 2024, 2025.

## 1. How ECZ sets Biology papers

**Paper 1 (5090/1): 40 multiple-choice questions, 50 minutes.** About half of
the questions have a figure. The figure is small, has numbered (1, 2, 3, 4) or
lettered (A–D) pointers, and the options are often a table:

- "Identify the parts numbered 1, 2 and 3": the options are a table with
  columns 1, 2, 3 and rows A–D.
- "Which labelled part …?": the options are the letters on the figure.
- Many questions read a graph, a table of results or an experiment set-up.

**Paper 2 (5090/2): 80 marks, 1 h 45 min.**

- **Section A (44 marks):** five short-answer questions, all compulsory, about
  9 marks each. Almost every question opens with "Figure n shows …". It then
  asks, in this order:
  1. identify the parts labelled P, Q, R (1 mark each);
  2. state the function or property of one labelled part;
  3. explain a process ("At the end of the experiment explain how … would have
     changed");
  4. apply the idea (a control experiment, a disorder, an adaptation).
- **Section B (36 marks):** five essays, answer any three, 12 marks each.
  - Each essay has two or three parts: "Describe…", "Explain…", "Compare and
    contrast…", "With named examples…", "Describe how you would demonstrate…".
  - Essays have no figures.
  - Essays often end with a health or society part: conservation, drugs,
    contraception, sexual harassment.
- **Genetics** appears every year. It gives a family tree or a statement
  ("a normal couple had a haemophilic son"), then asks for genotypes and
  "draw a genetic diagram" (4–6 marks).

**The marking schemes:**

- give one word per identified part;
- give "any two/three of: …" lists for functions;
- give full genetic diagrams in the form parents → gametes → offspring →
  phenotypes/ratio.

## 3. What the syllabus asks learners to draw or read

The syllabus asks for drawings where it says "drawing", "illustrating" or
"examining … structure". These are the figures a Biology paper can be built
on, by form.

**Form 1**

- the compound microscope (parts and functions; magnification);
- cells:
  - plant, animal, bacterial and fungal cells;
  - specialised cells: nerve, red and white blood cells, ciliated cell,
    muscle, sperm, root hair, palisade, guard cell, xylem, phloem;
- the male and female reproductive systems;
- Rhizopus and Mucor;
- tropisms and the choice chamber;
- ecosystems.

**Form 2**

- micrographs of palisade, spongy and epidermal tissue, blood, muscle and
  nerve cells;
- the detailed cell (electron microscope);
- the leaf, external and internal;
- root and stem transverse sections, monocot and dicot;
- xylem and phloem in longitudinal section;
- transpiration and the potometer;
- the flower, pollen tube and double fertilisation;
- seeds and fruits for dispersal;
- germination, hypogeal and epigeal;
- shoot and root tips;
- the life cycles of the housefly and the mosquito;
- food chains and food webs.

**Form 3**

- reagents and food tests;
- osmosis and plasmolysis;
- enzyme graphs and lock and key;
- teeth and dental formulae;
- the alimentary canal and the small intestine (villus);
- blood cells;
- the heart in longitudinal section, the cardiac cycle;
- double circulation;
- arteries, veins and capillaries;
- the lymphatic system, tissue fluid;
- breathing organs:
  - insects: spiracles and tracheae;
  - fish: gills;
  - humans: lungs and alveoli;
- respiration experiments;
- the frog life cycle;
- the menstrual cycle, the foetus and placenta;
- the endocrine glands;
- neurones, the reflex arc and the brain;
- the carbon, nitrogen and water cycles;
- dichotomous keys.

**Form 4**

- mitosis and meiosis stages;
- excretion: the urinary system, kidney and nephron, the skin;
- genetic diagrams and Punnett squares, including sex linkage and ABO;
- variation graphs: histograms for continuous variation, bar charts for
  discontinuous variation;
- the eye (and accommodation, short and long sight);
- the ear;
- skeletons:
  - the insect exoskeleton;
  - the human axial and appendicular skeleton;
  - vertebrae;
  - synovial, ball-and-socket and hinge joints;
  - biceps and triceps;
- adaptations: cactus, chameleon and others.

## 4. What the generator can draw today, and the gaps

**Already possible**

- `scene`: food-chain, ecological-pyramid (numbers, biomass, energy), the
  carbon, nitrogen, water and rock cycles.
- `chart`: bar charts and histograms of results; the variation graphs.
- `graph`: exact curves. It can draw an enzyme curve, but the AI has to invent
  a formula.
- `tree`: family trees for blood groups and haemophilia.
- `diagram` (Mermaid): the levels-of-organisation chain with blank boxes.
- `image`: any drawing the teacher has uploaded to the diagram library.
- Markdown tables: Punnett squares, tables of results, option tables for
  Paper 1.

**Missing.** These are the structures ECZ draws most:

- **Plant:** flower half-section, leaf T.S., root and stem T.S.
  (monocot/dicot), shoot and root tips, seed L.S., germination stages.
- **Cells:** plant, animal and bacterial cells, the electron-microscope cell,
  specialised cells, mitosis stages, osmosis in cells (turgid, plasmolysed,
  crenated).
- **Human organs:**
  - the alimentary canal, villus, tooth;
  - the heart, the double circulation loop, capillary with tissue fluid and
    lymph;
  - the lungs and alveolus;
  - the kidney and nephron, the skin;
  - the eye, the ear, the brain, the reflex arc;
  - the endocrine glands on a body outline;
  - the male and female reproductive systems, the foetus in the uterus;
  - the vertebra, a long bone, the elbow joint and its muscles.
- **Experiments:**
  - osmosis (thistle funnel, potato strips);
  - the potometer;
  - lime-water respiration flasks;
  - germination flasks;
  - choice chamber;
  - soil drainage funnels;
  - the microscope;
  - food tests (test tubes in a water bath).
- **Biology graphs and charts:**
  - enzyme activity against temperature or pH, one or two curves with the
    optimum marked;
  - S-shaped population growth;
  - body temperature against time;
  - the menstrual cycle as a 28-day circle with menstruation and ovulation
    marked.
- **Food web:** given organisms and "eats" links, drawn with arrows the ECZ way.
- **Dichotomous key:** drawn as numbered couplets.
- **Life cycles:** housefly, mosquito, frog.

## 5. Plan

Same approach as Geography. Each diagram is drawn by code (an SVG drawer in
`client/components/ai/figures/render/scenes/`) and listed in `sceneCatalog.ts`
with named parts. The AI letters or numbers the parts, and the server writes
the answers into the marking key. Diagrams take labels in two styles:

- **Paper 2 style:** letters P, Q, R.
- **Paper 1 style:** numbers 1, 2, 3 with a table of options.

Build in this order (most often examined first):

1. **Plant structure:**
   - flower (insect/wind, half-section, after fertilisation);
   - leaf T.S. and leaf external;
   - root and stem T.S. (monocot/dicot);
   - root hair cell, xylem and phloem;
   - seed L.S. and germination (epigeal/hypogeal);
   - shoot and root tips.
2. **Cells:**
   - plant, animal and bacterial cells, the electron-microscope cell;
   - specialised cells;
   - mitosis stages (interphase → telophase), meiosis;
   - osmosis in plant and animal cells.
3. **Human body:**
   - alimentary canal, villus, tooth (L.S. and decay);
   - heart, double circulation, blood vessels, capillary with tissue fluid,
     blood cells;
   - lungs and alveolus;
   - kidney and nephron, skin;
   - eye (with ray diagrams for short and long sight), ear, brain, reflex arc
     and spinal cord;
   - endocrine glands;
   - male and female reproductive systems, foetus in the uterus;
   - vertebra, long bone, synovial joint, biceps and triceps.
4. **Experiments:**
   - thistle-funnel osmometer, potato cylinders;
   - potometer;
   - lime-water respiration train;
   - germination flasks;
   - choice chamber;
   - soil drainage funnels;
   - compound microscope;
   - test tubes in a water bath.
5. **New figure types:**
   - `bio-curve`: enzyme against temperature or pH, population growth, body
     temperature. The server checks it and works out the optimum and the
     plateau.
   - `food-web`: organisms and links. The server works out producers,
     herbivores, food chains and trophic levels.
   - `menstrual-cycle`: the 28-day circle.
   - Genetics: a `genetic-cross` type that writes the parents → gametes →
     offspring diagram or the Punnett square. The server works the cross out
     (monohybrid, sex-linked, ABO), so the marking key is always correct.
6. **Life cycles:** housefly, mosquito, frog. Also Rhizopus, binary fission,
   budding and runners.
7. **AI instructions for Biology:**
   - Paper 1: numbered pointers plus a table of options.
   - Paper 2 Section A: figure, then identify, then function, then explain.
   - Section B: essays with no figures.
   - A genetics question every paper.
   - "Draw a genetic diagram" questions take a worked cross in the key.

Agricultural Science shares much of this (flower, leaf, root, seeds,
germination, soil experiments, the nitrogen cycle). The same drawers can be
reused there once its syllabus is in.

## 6. Human body reference: Color Atlas of Anatomy (Rohen, 7th ed.)

I checked the atlas against the human-body organs in the syllabus and the
papers. I studied the contents and the chapters that match them: skeleton,
joints, circulatory, lymphatic and nervous systems, teeth, brain, ear, eye,
vertebrae, lungs, heart, fetal circulation, digestive organs, kidney and
urinary system, reproductive organs, forearm, elbow, arm muscles, femur and
knee.

**How we use it:**

- The atlas is a book of photographs of dissected specimens. It is
  copyrighted and far more detailed than O Level.
- We use it only as a reference, to get shapes, positions and proportions
  right and to name parts correctly.
- Its pictures are **not** copied into the app or the diagram library.
- Our drawings stay simple black-and-white line diagrams, as ECZ uses. They
  label only the parts the syllabus names (see the table below).

**What the atlas does not cover.** These are microscopic and plant structures
the papers need, so we draw them from textbook conventions:

- cells and specialised cells, mitosis;
- villus, alveolus, nephron, neurone types;
- skin section;
- blood cells, capillary with tissue fluid;
- everything about plants;
- insects, life cycles and the experiment set-ups.

**ECZ drawing conventions to follow for the human figures:**

- A single outline drawing with light stippling, never shaded realism.
- **Pointer letters:**
  - Paper 2: pointer lines ending in capital letters (P, Q, R…), letters
    placed outside the drawing on the right or left;
  - Paper 1: numbers 1–4.
- Organs shown in their usual textbook view:
  - the heart from the front, with the person's left on the reader's right;
  - the kidney in longitudinal section;
  - the eye in horizontal section;
  - the ear as a section through the head;
  - the brain as a median section;
  - the female system from the front;
  - the male system from the side.
- One question letters three to five parts and asks for one function or
  property.

## 7. Drawing pass, 18 November 2026

Every Biology scene was rendered and checked by eye. Then the weakest ones
were redrawn and the missing ones the papers use most were added. All of them
are still plain black-and-white SVG drawn by code.

**Redrawn**

- **Heart** (`human1.ts`): new layout. The aorta rises out of the left
  ventricle and the pulmonary artery out of the right, without crossing the
  atria. The vena cavae and pulmonary veins open into the atria. The valves
  have flaps, tendons and papillary muscles. Arrows show the blood flow
  through all four chambers. The right ventricle wall is thin and the left
  is thick.
- **Ear** (`human2.ts`): drawn as a section through the skull bone, with no
  grey box. The three ossicles are separate shapes, each one identifiable
  (hammer, anvil, stirrup in the oval window). The cochlea is a coiled tube,
  there are three semicircular canals, and the Eustachian tube runs to the
  throat.
- **Foetus in the uterus**: the foetus is curled head-down with its head,
  limbs and neck drawn. The umbilical cord is twisted. The placenta has
  villi. The cervix has a mucus plug.
- **Arm (biceps and triceps)**: the elbow is bent. Each muscle has a tendon
  at both ends: the biceps on the radius, the triceps on the point of the
  ulna. The hinge is marked.
- **Reflex arc**: drawn the textbook way, with single-line neurones, a
  ganglion on the dorsal root and the grey-matter butterfly. Arrows show the
  impulse direction.
- **Guard cells**: real epidermal cells all round the pair (irregular, wavy
  walls), replacing the stray lines.
- **Osmosis in animal cells**: the burst cell now shows a torn membrane with
  cytoplasm escaping.
